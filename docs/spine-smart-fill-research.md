# Front-cover recognition: wording and detection research

Research date: 2026-10-03. Scope: French, Arabic, English, and mixed-language academic covers, with priority to Tunisian and North African examples.

The initial research below informed a lightweight local implementation, described at the end of this document. The sources establish that wording and layouts vary; they do not establish how frequently each expression appears among customers of this shop. Observed phrases below were checked in university documents. Additional candidates are linguistic extensions requiring validation.

## Main finding

Recognize the relationship between a label and its text block, rather than choosing the first person, largest heading, or first year on the page. Separate student authors, supervisors, examiners, institutional officers, research titles, degree descriptions, and dates before mapping anything to spine fields.

The Tunisian ISEAH Mahdia guide is particularly useful: it includes three language templates for the same institution. The Arabic template places students on the right and supervision on the left; the French and English versions reverse those columns. Their sample footer gives a month and one year, rather than an academic-year range. The template sheets are images: ordinary PDF text extraction did not recover their labels. [ISEAH Mahdia guide, physical PDF pages 39, 41, 43](https://iseahm.rnu.tn/userfiles/files/GIDE%20PFE%20ISEAHM%202025-2026.pdf)

## Proposed detection rules

These are engineering conclusions from the examples, not measured model performance.

1. **Start with the selected front page.** Do not collect names from dedications, acknowledgments, institutional instructions, signatures, or body paragraphs. If the selected page is a guide/template rather than a completed cover, report missing data. An optional inner title page can supplement evidence, with its page recorded.
2. **Retain positions and reading direction.** Extract PDF text with coordinates and render a sufficiently clear page image. Reconstruct separate columns. Never assume that authors are always on the left, on the right, or immediately after the label in the flattened text stream.
3. **Associate whole blocks.** Attach names to their local label, stop at another role boundary, and recognize jury-table rows. Academic and industry supervisors can coexist. A supervisor can reappear in a jury table without becoming another author.
4. **Keep all authors.** Preserve an array of names before choosing how to display them on the spine. Do not split compound names on arbitrary spaces, hyphens, Ben/Bin/Ibn, بن, or عبد. A connector such as et/and/و is only a separator when the surrounding block supports separate identities. Repeated equivalent names across language blocks are not automatically different people.
5. **Separate research topic from document and degree headings.** PFE, mémoire, thèse, dissertation, master's thesis, مذكرة, أطروحة, and degree names describe the work, not necessarily its title. Preserve subtitles and case-study lines when they are part of the topic. A phrase can occur inside the actual title, so no global removal list.
6. **Do not invent a short title.** First extract the complete printed title. Let the existing spine auto-fit use it. Any shortening should be a separately reviewed suggestion that preserves meaning.
7. **Classify dates first.** Prefer an explicitly labeled academic year for the current spine field. Store submission/defense/graduation dates separately. If only a single year is printed, present that with its source type; never manufacture a range from the current year or file date. Keep Gregorian and Hijri candidates separate and require a choice if neither is designated for the spine.
8. **Normalize only the matching representation.** Ignore label case, French accents, repeated whitespace, colons, and typographic apostrophe differences. For Arabic label matching, handle presentation forms, tatweel, diacritics, hamza variants, and explicit student gender/number suffixes. Map Arabic-Indic and Persian digits to a comparison form. Preserve original title and name spelling for display; never silently transliterate or 'correct' a person's name.
9. **Use bounded tolerance for damaged labels.** A damaged spelling such as إشراؼ can be a supervision candidate in the right block. Fuzzy matching belongs to the label, not the name; it must not merge distinct people. Word boundaries and local context matter.
10. **Cross-check text against the image.** The inspected Ghardaia cover visibly contains an academic-year pair, while the extracted text returned repeated malformed digits. The ISEAH template labels are absent from text extraction altogether. Missing or corrupted text is a reason for image reading or review, not guessing.
11. **Record evidence for every proposed value.** Keep source page, printed label, quoted value, and block/crop reference alongside alternatives and reasons for uncertainty. A model's own confidence score is not an accuracy measurement. Conflicting roles, unreadable names, and mismatching dates should require review.
12. **Preserve manual choices.** Show proposed fields and support applying selected values. Do not overwrite edited spine fields during a page change, rerun, or delayed response. Results must remain associated with the front page that was analyzed.

## Evaluation plan before enabling automatic filling

Build a manually labeled set drawn from actual shop covers and university templates, with a separate evaluation subset that is not used to adjust rules. A proposed initial target is 50-100 varied covers, not a completed dataset. Include all three languages, bilingual pages, independent front PDFs, scans, image-only pages, multiple authors, and different institutions. Anonymize customer fixtures used in repository tests.

Measure author extraction, supervisor-to-author confusion, exact title preservation, date/calendar classification, missing-field detection, and how often human review is requested. Count abstaining separately from wrong automatic fills. Choose any auto-fill threshold from evaluated errors rather than a self-reported probability. Zero wrong automatic fills in a small test set would still not prove universal accuracy.

## Implemented lightweight autofill

The selected front-cover PDF now triggers detection automatically, filling the existing spine year, title, and student-name fields. Those fields remain editable. The existing editor also has a retry button; it does not add a separate detected-text form.

PDF cover files can be dragged into the active Hardcover workspace, or onto the existing whole-book/front/back upload buttons. One file dropped elsewhere becomes the front cover. A clearly named front/back pair is imported sequentially regardless of input order; ambiguous pairs, folders, empty files, and non-PDF formats are rejected before source replacement. File drops cannot navigate the app away. Shared source-operation locking prevents imports from racing page selection, and leaving the tool releases any source registered by an import that finishes afterward. Browser checks cover drops, retained front artwork when adding the back, existing file pickers, automatic multiline spine fill, and preservation of source/spine data after invalid drops.

Detected titles retain the printed line boundaries, including a subtitle on a second line after a colon or other punctuation. Inline punctuation alone does not introduce a break. The existing title field is a multiline editor, and the spine fitter preserves explicit source/manual lines while reducing font size or showing the existing fit warning. Preview, SVG, and PDF export share those lines. A PDF proof from the supplied architecture cover verifies the main title and subtitle on two separate lines; no new field or recognition model is added.

Auto-fit typography scales with both spine width and usable book height around the standard A4 reference, with a 6-36 pt automatic range. Per-item length and width limits still take precedence, so a longer subtitle may remain smaller than the year/name when its available length is already full. The existing size badge shows the range actually used. Measurement changes resize and recenter text without rerunning recognition or changing wording/explicit line breaks; manual sizing remains available when Auto-fit is off.

The fixed 46% middle allocation was subsequently removed: the title now uses the free length between the year and name. A group fit scales the preferred sizes if total text would exceed the available length, and retains end insets and gaps between sections. With the supplied two-line architecture title, widening from 20 to 30 mm changes its font from 16 to 19.6 pt together with the year/name. Regression checks verify the original two lines, growth/shrinkage, end boundaries, and non-overlap of the estimated text blocks; preview and PDF use the same layout.

The default path reads the PDF text layer and uses bounded layout and label rules. OCR starts only for scanned or damaged-text pages. It is a lazy module with bundled Arabic/French/English data, one temporary worker, a maximum 1800-pixel page edge, a 90-second timeout, immediate cancellation when the source changes, and cleanup afterward. An optional small author-column retry uses the same worker. It is accepted only when it identifies a complete valid author result.

Detection preserves user edits on automatic page changes and rejects late results for a different source. Explicit retries may replace existing values that are successfully detected, but do not erase unresolved manual text. A cover with missing information clears untouched placeholders/old automatically detected values rather than leaving an invented current year or the previous book's details. Arabic dual-author labels require both authors to be identified before a name is filled.

Recognition assets load from the installed app. The language data is embedded in the lazy module as data URLs to avoid download-manager interception of archive requests and file-protocol restrictions. The three small core variants select the customer's available SIMD support. No CDN, paid service, resident model, or GPU dependency is used. OCR assets add approximately 19 MB of uncompressed installed files; they are not loaded at app startup. The compressed installer increase has not been measured.
